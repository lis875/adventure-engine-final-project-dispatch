Your skull throbs before the rest of your body catches up to consciousness. A sharp, needle-like cold presses against your spine. When you finally force your eyes open, the world is nothing but black shapes and colder shadows. The air tastes damp, metallic, and strangely familiar like a memory you’re not brave enough to claim.
You shift, and the wooden bed beneath you complains with a low groan.
 Dust falls from the headboard in delicate strands, drifting like ash.
For several long seconds, you do not breathe.
You do not trust what might happen if you do.
Then your hand brushes something cylindrical — hard, cold, and undeniably mechanical.
 A flashlight.
You click it on.
The beam trembles with weakness, but it is enough to reveal the room: a warped wardrobe hunched in the corner like a patient predator, peeling wallpaper curled like dead petals, and two doors, both closed, both waiting.
